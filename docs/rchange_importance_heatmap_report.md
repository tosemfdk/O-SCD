**FP suppression signal exists, it is modest, and it is entirely made of
observation support. Agreement actively hurts; observability contributes
nothing measurable.**

rank TP pixels above FP pixels. Baseline prevalence (fraction of the prediction

## The exponent sweep is unusually clean

Marginal mean AUPRC over all 192 combinations of
`I = m^γ · [ε+(1−ε)S]^α · [ε+(1−ε)A]^β · [ε+(1−ε)O]^δ` (ε = 0.05):

```
γ (belief m)     0.5: 0.8221   1.0: 0.8203   2.0: 0.8166
α (support S)    0.0: 0.8178   0.5: 0.8204   1.0: 0.8206   2.0: 0.8200
β (agreement A)  0.0: 0.8283   0.5: 0.8223   1.0: 0.8176   2.0: 0.8106
δ (observ.  O)   0.0: 0.8197   0.5: 0.8197   1.0: 0.8197   2.0: 0.8197
```

- **β is monotonically harmful.** Every increase in the agreement exponent costs
  AUPRC. All 15 top-ranked sweep variants have β = 0.
- **δ is flat to four decimals.** Observability, as the normalized logdet of the
  accumulated 3×3 position block, carries no usable information here.
- **α helps and saturates** by 0.5–1.0.
- **γ < 1 helps.** Note this is not a no-op even though AUPRC is rank-based: the
  rendered map is a *linear* composite `M_I(p) = Σ_g r_g,p I_g`, so compressing
  per-Gaussian values changes pixel ordering. A compressive γ stops a few
  high-belief Gaussians from dominating the mixture.

## Why observability is dead here (and what to use instead)

The position blocks come from the xyz-adjoint of the **change** render, which
vanishes wherever `c` is flat. Consequently **48.7–83.0% of Gaussians per scene
(mean ≈ 70%) sit exactly on the damping floor** and get `O = 0` by construction.
`I5`/`I6` therefore hard-zero most of the model; only the ε-floored sweep keeps
them alive.

The estimator itself is *not* the problem. Hutchinson noise was the risk we
flagged before running; the split-half check (two independent 64-probe halves,
measured on the informative subset — a whole-population correlation is inflated
to 1.000 by the tied floor mass) gives **ρ ≥ 0.9996 in every scene**. The block
*magnitude* is deterministic; only its direction is stochastic, so logdet is
reproducible. 128 probes are far more than needed.

But the Gaussian-space attribution says the geometric axis is not worthless —
it is being read through the wrong functional:

```
scalar          rho(TP purity)   AUROC(TP-supported)
n_dir_obs           +0.246              0.629
support S           +0.208              0.592
agreement A         +0.177              0.607
confidence          +0.148              0.562
c_raw               +0.081              0.583
m                   +0.063              0.555
g_raw (logdet)      -0.055              0.466
observability O     -0.044              0.469
uncertainty U       -0.215              0.351
condition number    -0.227              0.362
```

**The change belief barely predicts whether a Gaussian's pixels are real change
(`m`: ρ = +0.063).** What predicts it is how often and how well the Gaussian was
observed (`n_dir_obs` +0.246, `S` +0.208) and how badly conditioned its position
block is (`condition` −0.227 — the strongest negative signal in the table).
Conditioning, not logdet magnitude, is where the geometry lives.

(This is alpha-compositing attribution, not a 3D GT label: a Gaussian counts as
TP-supported when the pixels it is responsible for are mostly true positives.
Primary conclusions are image-space; this table is causal reading only.)

**Zen — the predicted win, confirmed.** Precision at 95% recall goes
0.675 → 0.792 (+11.7pp), the largest gain of any scene. Its flagged-toxic frames
(3, 4, 6) have a prediction that is only **27.3% precise** (vs 66.3% on the rest
of the scene), and importance still reaches AUPRC 0.888 on them — i.e. the FP
mass in the toxic frames is genuinely separable by state. Zen-type toxicity is
visible in the Gaussian state.

**Porch — the predicted failure, confirmed.** It is the only scene where no
importance definition beats raw `c` (−0.0116). Its toxic frames (0, 7) are
*cleaner* than the rest of the scene (prevalence 0.847 vs 0.708), so there is
nothing for a state-space filter to find: the damage is not "this frame's cue is
wrong", it is viewpoint/3D, exactly as Part 3 concluded from the cue montages.

**Printing_area — invisible to every axis.** Toxic frame 4 is statistically
indistinguishable from the rest (AUPRC 0.961 vs 0.959, prevalence 0.899 vs
0.888). Whatever makes it toxic is not in support, agreement, observability, or
belief.

**Garden — the render-failure FP survives everything.** Frame 23's lower-left
FP blob (the 3DGS floor corner the reference render fails on) is bright in `m`,
`S`, `A`, `O` *and* `Conf` — see
`montages/state_frame_23.png`. Not one state axis suppresses it. Garden still
posts the largest AUPRC lift (+0.063) because its *other* FP — the halo — is
suppressed. Frame 15 (clean render) behaves as expected: importance concentrates
on the TP cores.

## Honest limitations

1. **Part of the apparent FP rejection is just alpha.** `M_I = Σ_g r_g,p I_g` is
   responsibility-weighted, so pixels with little Gaussian coverage score low
   regardless of importance. Dividing by the alpha map removes that channel and
   costs AUPRC everywhere (raw → alpha: I0 −0.033, I2 −0.032, S0 −0.077). The
   raw composite is the primary metric per §11, but the gap is the size of the
   coverage confound, and it is comparable to the entire gain over baseline.
   Both norms are in `variant_metrics.csv`.
2. **The gain is small in absolute terms.** +0.014 AUPRC (best required variant
   over raw `c`), +0.019 for the best sweep (0.8350). Against a prevalence of 0.656 the
   headline numbers look high; the lift over the baseline is what to judge.
3. **The cue is clamped.** `soft_counts` requires a [0,1] mask while C_v reaches
   2.0; ~2.1% of pixels per frame are clipped (recorded per frame in each
   `manifest.json`). No re-normalization was applied, per §3.
4. **One seed.** seed 0 only; scene-level run-to-run noise is ±0.005 mIoU in the
   pipeline and was not re-measured here.
5. **Gaussian-space numbers are responsibility attribution**, not 3D ground
   truth.

## Recommendations (§22)

1. **Best TP preservation:** `I1_beta_mean` (m) — highest top-10% precision
   (0.8939) and second AUPRC, but the worst FP rejection of the useful group.
   If the downstream cost of a dropped TP dominates, use plain `m`.
2. **Best FP suppression:** `S0_support` with κ₀ = 8 — best precision at both
   recall 0.95 (0.7329) and 0.99 (0.7058), best FP rejection (0.2962), and it
   never looks at the cue. The combined `I2 = m·S` is the best all-rounder
   (top AUPRC 0.8295) and is what the montages use.
3. **Zen-type and Porch-type toxicity cannot be handled by one expression.**
   Zen's is separable in state space (+11.7pp precision); Porch's is not
   detectable at all (−0.012 AUPRC, and its toxic frames are its *cleanest*).
   A single importance term will keep failing on Porch. Porch-type damage has to
   be attacked at frame selection / geometry, not at per-Gaussian filtering.
4. **Keep importance and verification as separate terms — confirmed
   empirically.** `V0_verification` (0.6275) and `U0_uncertainty` (0.6260) score
   *below* prevalence (0.656), i.e. they are mildly anti-correlated with being a
   true positive. That is the correct behaviour for a "what should I look at
   next" signal and proves it must never be used as an importance weight.
5. **Carry forward to the next selector experiment:**
   - `I2_mean_support = m·S` (best overall separation)
   - `sweep_g0.5_a0.5_b0_d0` — i.e. `m^0.5 · S^0.5`, agreement and observability
     dropped (best sweep family; δ is provably inert, so prefer the cheaper form)
   - a **conditioning-based** geometric term to replace O: the condition number
     of the position block is the strongest negative correlate of TP purity
     (−0.227) while its logdet is inert (−0.055). This is a new candidate, not a
     validated one.
